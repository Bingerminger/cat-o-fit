# Methods & sources

**English** · [Deutsch](../de/knowledge/methods-and-sources.md)

Which method is behind which number – and where it comes from. Cat-O-Fit calculates with published,
verifiable methods; where the research supports less than the reputation of a figure suggests, the app
says so.

## Training and load

| Figure in Cat-O-Fit | Method | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Current form, prediction, training pace | VDOT (equivalence of performance via oxygen uptake) | Daniels J: *Daniels' Running Formula*, 3rd ed., Human Kinetics 2013 (tables after Daniels & Gilbert, *Oxygen Power*, 1979) |
| Prediction without hard runs | Riegel formula T₂ = T₁ · (D₂/D₁)^1.06 | Riegel PS: Athletic records and human endurance. *American Scientist* 69 (1981), 285–290 |
| Fitness, fatigue, form | Fitness-fatigue model (exponential smoothing, 42 and 7 days) | Banister EW et al.: A systems model of training for athletic performance. *Aust J Sports Med* 7 (1975), 57–61 |
| Load points | Session RPE: minutes × effort | Foster C et al.: A new approach to monitoring exercise training. *J Strength Cond Res* 15 (2001), 109–115 |
| Monotony and strain | Weekly average ÷ standard deviation of the daily load | Foster C: Monitoring training in athletes with reference to overtraining syndrome. *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 30 (1998), 1164–1168 |
| Load ratio (ACWR) | 7 days against the 21 days before, **uncoupled** | Hulin BT et al., *Br J Sports Med* 50 (2016), 231–236; Gabbett TJ, *Br J Sports Med* 50 (2016), 273–280 |
| … and its limits | No injury prediction, no promise of protection; uncoupled calculation | Lolli L et al.: Mathematical coupling causes spurious correlation within the conventional acute-to-chronic workload ratio calculations. *Br J Sports Med* 53 (2019), 921–922; Impellizzeri FM et al.: Acute:chronic workload ratio: conceptual issues and fundamental pitfalls. *Int J Sports Physiol Perform* 15 (2020), 907–913 |
| Max HR estimate | 208 − 0.7 × age | Tanaka H, Monahan KD, Seals DR: Age-predicted maximal heart rate revisited. *J Am Coll Cardiol* 37 (2001), 153–156 |
| HR zones with resting heart rate | Heart rate reserve | Karvonen MJ, Kentala E, Mustala O: The effects of training on heart rate. *Ann Med Exp Biol Fenn* 35 (1957), 307–315 |
| HR zones from threshold HR | Zones as a percentage of lactate-threshold HR; field test: average HR of the last 20 of 30 minutes | Friel J: *The Triathlete's Training Bible*, 4th ed., VeloPress 2016 |
| Effort from heart rate (without your own rating) | Average HR as a percentage of max HR → level on the 0–10 scale; only an input value for the load points, your own rating takes precedence | Intensity levels after Garber CE et al.: ACSM Position Stand: Quantity and quality of exercise for developing and maintaining cardiorespiratory, musculoskeletal, and neuromotor fitness in apparently healthy adults. *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 43 (2011), 1334–1359 |
| HRV in readiness | 7-day mean of the logarithmised value against your own 28-day range (mean ± 0.5 standard deviations) | Plews DJ et al.: Training adaptation and heart rate variability in elite endurance athletes: opening the door to effective monitoring. *Sports Med* 43 (2013), 773–781; Buchheit M: Monitoring training status with HR measures: do all roads lead to Rome? *Front Physiol* 5 (2014), 73 |
| Elevation gain in the form estimate | Rule of thumb: from 5 m of climbing per km, every metre climbed counts like 6 m on the flat | based on the energy cost of uphill running: Minetti AE et al.: Energy cost of walking and running at extreme uphill and downhill slopes. *J Appl Physiol* 93 (2002), 1039–1046 |
| Heat hint (“about …% slower than planned”) | Rule of thumb: from a daily high of 20 °C, about 0.4% per degree above 15 °C, at most 8% | based on Ely MR et al.: Impact of weather on marathon-running performance. *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 39 (2007), 487–493 |
| Strength: next step | first raise the repetitions up to 12, then the weight (double progression) | Ratamess NA et al.: ACSM Position Stand: Progression models in resistance training for healthy adults. *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 41 (2009), 687–708 |
| Weekly goals, programmes | 150–300 minutes of endurance per week, strength on two days | WHO: *Guidelines on physical activity and sedentary behaviour*, Geneva 2020 |

## Energy and nutrition

| Figure in Cat-O-Fit | Method | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Basal metabolic rate (from age 19) | Mifflin-St Jeor | Mifflin MD, St Jeor ST et al.: A new predictive equation for resting energy expenditure in healthy individuals. *Am J Clin Nutr* 51 (1990), 241–247 |
| Energy availability, threshold 30 | (energy − exercise expenditure) ÷ fat-free mass | Loucks AB, Thuma JR: Luteinizing hormone pulsatility is disrupted at a threshold of energy availability in regularly menstruating women. *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* 88 (2003), 297–311 |
| RED-S, lower limit for men (25) | Classification, not a diagnosis | Mountjoy M et al.: 2023 International Olympic Committee's (IOC) consensus statement on Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (REDs). *Br J Sports Med* 57 (2023), 1073–1097 |
| Protein 1.2–2.0 g per kg | Guideline for people who exercise | Thomas DT, Erdman KA, Burke LM: Nutrition and Athletic Performance (position statement of the ACSM, the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics and Dietitians of Canada). *J Acad Nutr Diet* 116 (2016), 501–528 |
| Exercise expenditure (net in the balance, gross in the display) | Runs by distance (about 1 kcal per kg and km), other sports by MET values × body weight × duration, based on the Compendium; net of the everyday expenditure of the same time | Ainsworth BE et al.: 2011 Compendium of Physical Activities. *Med Sci Sports Exerc* 43 (2011), 1575–1581 |
| Exercise expenditure from the watch | where Apple Health supplies active calories, they count instead of the estimate (net as above); energy availability says whether measured, partly measured or estimated | Watches estimate too – with noticeable deviation: Shcherbina A et al.: Accuracy in wrist-worn, sensor-based measurements of heart rate and energy expenditure in a diverse cohort. *J Pers Med* 7 (2017), 3 |
| Nutrition values of the ingredients | curated, rounded average values per 100 g or ml; optionally Open Food Facts (also by barcode) | Open Food Facts (ODbL), see [CREDITS](../../CREDITS.md) |

## Lab values

Every value also names its source in the app – in the detail view under “Basis of the ranges”.
Principle: **your lab's reference range takes precedence**; the sport target corridors are a second,
sport-related layer.

| Topic | Source |
|---|---|
| Sport corridor for ferritin and transferrin saturation | Peeling P, Sim M, McKay AKA: Iron status in sport (German edition: *Eisenstatus im Sport*), Gatorade Sports Science Institute (GSSI), *Sports Science Exchange* |
| Ferritin with inflammation (CRP from the same blood draw) | WHO: *Guideline on use of ferritin concentrations to assess iron status in individuals and populations*, Geneva 2020 |
| Anaemia cut-offs for haemoglobin (12.0 / 13.0 / 11.0 g/dl) | WHO: *Guideline on haemoglobin cutoffs to define anaemia in individuals and populations*, Geneva 2024 |
| Following up a high transferrin saturation | EASL Clinical Practice Guidelines on haemochromatosis. *J Hepatol* 77 (2022), 479–502 |
| Vitamin D sufficient from 50 nmol/l; no consensus on higher targets | DGE reference value for vitamin D (2012; German Nutrition Society); Maughan RJ et al., IOC consensus 2018 (see below); Demay MB et al.: Vitamin D for the Prevention of Disease (Endocrine Society). *J Clin Endocrinol Metab* 109 (2024), 1907–1947 |
| Classification of holo-TC | LADR laboratory network (below 35 deficiency likely, 35–50 grey area) |
| CRP, CK and urea after endurance exercise | Thomas L (ed.): *Labor und Diagnose* (German laboratory-medicine reference), ch. 51; IQWiG, gesundheitsinformation.de (CRP) |
| fT3, testosterone, oestradiol as a sign of low energy | IOC consensus 2023 (REDs), see above |
| A trend only counts above natural variation (RCV) | EFLM Biological Variation Database (biologicalvariation.eu); biological variation of ferritin according to *Scand J Clin Lab Invest* 2023 |
| Magnesium, whole blood and serum | usual ranges of large German laboratories (named per value in the app) |

> Cat-O-Fit serves **documentation and general information for healthy adults**. The sources explain
